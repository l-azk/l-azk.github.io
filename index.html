Shadows & Objecthood

What is the relationship between shadows and their objects in visual processing?

Shadows are unusual. They carry useful information about the objects that cast them, but they can change shape, stretch across different surfaces, and even become physically separated from their objects. This raises a basic question: does the visual system treat a shadow as something separate, or does it somehow bind the shadow to the object that casts it?

To test this, we measure how easily attention moves between objects, shadows, and surrounding surfaces. Across experiments, we find that attention moves between a shadow and its casting object as if the usual boundary between separate objects is absent—even when the shadow is distorted or physically separated from its object. We suggest that shadows may therefore be derivative individuals: distinct from their casting objects, but nevertheless bound together with them in visual processing.

AI & Romantic Relationships

What happens when AI becomes a third voice in a disagreement?

People increasingly turn to AI not only for information, but also for advice about situations involving other people. That raises a different kind of question from whether an AI response is simply accurate or useful: can the perspective an AI provides change the way we understand someone close to us?

In this project, I study conflict between romantic partners and compare AI feedback that supports a person’s existing interpretation with feedback that challenges it. The broader goal is to understand whether AI can shift perspective-taking, emotional responses, and the way people subsequently approach interpersonal disagreement.

AI & Apologies

If AI writes the better apology, would you actually use it?

Generative AI can produce polished interpersonal messages almost instantly. But an apology is not judged only by how well it is written. Its value can also depend on whether the recipient believes it reflects the apologizer’s own thoughts, effort, and intentions.

I’m interested in the tension between quality and authorship: when people compare an apology they wrote themselves with one generated by AI, what determines which message they are willing to send? This provides a way to study how AI changes judgments of authenticity in communication where sincerity matters.

Visual Cognition Lab · UBC

March 2025–present

Visual cognition · Information perception

Seeing Correlation

How does the visual system extract statistical structure from visually different displays?

A scatterplot can represent the same statistical relationship while looking very different depending on how its individual points are rendered. We examine whether changing low-level visual properties—such as the size, shape, and colour of the dots—changes how strongly people perceive the underlying correlation.

Our findings so far suggest that perceived correlation is remarkably robust to these changes. Despite differences in the appearance of individual elements, observers recover similar estimates of the higher-order relationship between them. This suggests that the visual system can extract abstract statistical structure while remaining relatively insensitive to variation in the features that make up the display.
